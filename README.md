# All About MATHS Blog

This blog is for mathematics enthusiasts who enjoy exploring different branches of mathematics and discovering the ideas that connect them.

<br>

## About me
Here is Peter Fayez, and I am a mathematical physics enthusiast, aspiring mechatronics engineer, with a strong interest in computational physics. 

I became fascinated by mathematics, particularly abstract algebra (including group and ring theory), calculus, and analysis, especially real analysis. As a result, I decided to explore mathematics systematically by studying and writing introductory content for different branches and subfields. I began to seriously study mathematics in Grade 9, and now I am entering Grade 12, preparing for US college admissions. My interests lie at the intersection of computational physics and mechatronics, with a particular interest in AI for embedded systems. Driven by these interests, I have completed several university-level mathematics courses, including Single Variable Calculus (MIT OCW), Linear Algebra (MIT OCW), and Multi-variable Calculus (MIT OCW) and Thomas Calculus, and also Differential equations, and now Mathematical Physics (Zewail OCW)& Topology. I am particularly interested in applying mathematical knowledge to Computational physics, including concepts that may initially seem highly abstract, such as number theory applications in dynamical systems, chaos theory, and non-linear dynamics. <em><strong>Yes!!!, Number theory has applications in physics-especially in dynamical systems and numerical analysis-; it isn't just an abstract subject </em></strong>

<br>

## Why I made this Blog?
While studying mathematics, I often looked for blogs that connected mathematical concepts with physics, while also providing accessible introductions to different branches of mathematics. Since I could not find exactly the kind of resource I was looking for, I decided to create one myself. However, this is still under construction. 

This project is still under development. If you are interested in contributing—whether through mathematical content, academic discussions, or web development—I would be grateful for your help.
